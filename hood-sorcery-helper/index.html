The Premise

Wizards here, dragons there, knights and paladins that. All the typical medieval fantasy elements one would come to expect from the genre. Except this time, it’s not the middle of the dark ages. We’re going modern. Only, a little less typical.

High-rises with dwarfs? Beachside resorts with mermaids? Dungeon crawling with the oh-so-typical adventurer group, only this time with M4s and a pack of dubiously acquired Semtex? Well, the first two were pretty typical, but the last one was quite interesting… eh, too late to change genres now! We’re doing Gang Wars!

What’s the story? What’s the background? What’s the reason for strife and conflict? Who cares!

We’ve got sorcerers, and they’ve got their backers. Only instead of being “ethereal” and “otherworldly”, they want a slice of that magical pie! And when you want something good, chances are you’re not the only one. So ready up your contraband, do your rituals with your crew, and get ready for some HOOD SORCERY!

The Vein & the Turf

Alright, alright, here’s some more lore. Veins, areas of high magical concentration, appear abruptly and with little warning, and go just as fast. They’re about as well understood as the first time Marie Curses started dabbling in hex patterns (Gods bless her soul shards). But with enough magical density to power a whole quadrant of a big high-rise city, everybody and their ethereal matriarch wants one, and nobody’s willing to share.

So who gets to fight over what? The Turfs: an assortment of like-minded individuals who, with great reverence towards their very, very benevolent masters, are thrown into the meat grinder to fight over even a sliver of control of the Veins around the world. In exchange for their lives (which are generally not very long), they get the ability to cast elements at will from the Vein itself, using their fingers as reusable alchemical “cartridges”. It’s rough, dirty work, but hey, you could cast a fireball and squirt water from your fingertips! Just make sure your thumbs are all in one piece. You’ll need them…

Goal

Four crews fight over one Vein. The Vein lasts 8 rounds; when it closes, the crew with the least damage takes it. The Fortune Wheel is the Vein acting up each round.

Every round, everyone casts at once, then ranks on Attack and on Block. A low rank costs damage.

§2Setup

  1. Seats. P1 Ember Saints (red, Fire), P2 Tide Syndicate (blue, Water), P3 Stone Crows (green, Earth), P4 Gale Runners (purple, Wind). Each player takes their standee, card holder, 3 sliders, 2 matrix cubes and 2 Trigger Thumbs (d6 side up).
  2. Set every slider to 0. Put the round marker on 1.
  3. Shuffle the spell deck and deal 8 cards to each player.

§3Spell cards

Each card shows a start element, an end element, its length (2, 3 or 4) and its ATK and BLK values.

Longer cards are stronger: ATK + BLK = 3 on a 2-length card, 4 on a 3-length card, 5 on a 4-length card. Cards always read left to right (start → end). They can't be turned around.

Browse all 72 in the Spell Deck.

§4The round

  1. Spin. Flick the Wheel. Its effect applies to everyone this round.
  2. Cast (secret, all at once). Lay 1–3 cards face down in a row. You may put 1 or 2 ready Trigger Thumbs beside your cast.
  3. Flip. Everyone reveals at once. Roll a d6 for each Trigger Thumb you committed (§4.2), then flip those tokens to SPENT. Set your ATK and BLK sliders.
  4. Rank. Rank everyone on ATK and on BLK (ties: §5). Put one cube on your ATK row and one on your BLK column. The cell where they cross is your damage. Move your DMG slider up by that much.
  5. Refill. Discard the cards you cast and draw back up to 8. Reset ATK and BLK to 0. If the deck runs out, shuffle the discards. Move the round marker on.

Your hand is always 8 cards. You can't discard from your hand: every card is either cast or kept.

§4.1The line: 8 fingers

Your cast is your line. Total length minus matched seams must be 8 or less.

A seam matches when one card ends on the same element the next card starts on. Each matched seam gives:

  • or : +1 ATK
  • or : +1 BLK
  • −1 length (the two ends share a finger)

Cards that don't match may sit side by side. They count their full length and give no bonus.

Examples: 4 + 4 = 8, legal. 4 + 4 + 2 with two seams = 8, legal. 3 + 3 + 3 with one seam = 8, legal. 4 + 4 + 4 with two seams = 10, too long.

Your totals = card values + seam bonuses + Wheel, then Trigger Thumbs. Totals stay between 0 and 15.

§4.2Trigger Thumbs

2 per player for the whole game. Commit 0, 1 or 2 face down with your cast. After the flip, roll a d6 for each one. Both results apply. Spent thumbs are gone.

§5Rank and damage

Highest total = 1st. Ties: 1) the shorter line wins; 2) then the higher other total; 3) then a d6 roll-off among the tied players (repeat until every place is unique).

Example: P1, P2, P3 all on 9 ATTACK with lines 8, 8, 7 → P3 is best of the three. P1 and P2 both have 6 BLOCK → roll: P1 5, P2 2 → P1 ahead.

Rows = ATK rank, columns = BLK rank, cell = damage taken.

§7End of game

After round 8, the player with the least damage wins.

Ties (any place): less damage taken in round 8; then higher ATK + BLK in round 8 (still on the sliders); then a roll-off.

The DAMAGE ladder tops out at 15+. Past 15, note it on the Score Sheet.

§8Quick answers

  • Cards that don't match? Allowed. Full length, no seam bonus.
  • A card with 0 ATK? Fine. It's a specialist: it wins one ranking and feeds the other card's stat.
  • Thumbs in Flip the Script? Roll first, then everyone decides whether to swap.
  • Swap Meet timing? Look at your hand, pass 2, then cast with what you received.
  • Thumb 6 when I took 0 damage? The −1 is wasted. It never lowers earlier rounds.
  • Hand full of 2-length cards? Cast a cheap round to cycle them (you refill to 8), or gamble a Thumb.
  • Bleeding? After tie-breaks there is exactly one last place on each stat.
  • The Turf / Cuffed and ties? “Shorter line” means the line you actually cast.
  • Turn a card around? No. Cards always read left to right.
